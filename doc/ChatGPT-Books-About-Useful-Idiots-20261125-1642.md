Yes. There is a recurring literary and cinematic theme that corresponds very closely to what Bezmenov describes: **the revolutionary, collaborator or traitor who helps bring a new regime to power, only to be eliminated by the very people he helped.**

The underlying logic is that someone willing to betray the old order may also become a threat to the new one, especially once their ideological illusions collapse.

## Names for this phenomenon

There are several established expressions and narrative tropes that may help you find further examples:

- **The revolution devours its own children.**
- **You Have Outlived Your Usefulness** - a narrative trope involving the elimination of expendable collaborators.
- **Rewarded as a Traitor Deserves** - a trope in which a traitor is betrayed or killed by those he helped.
- **The Great Purge** - the historical example of Stalinist political consolidation.
- **Revolutionary purges** - the broader political phenomenon.
- **The law of the jackal** - the idea that a collaborator becomes expendable after serving a stronger party's interests, though this is not a formal political-science term.
